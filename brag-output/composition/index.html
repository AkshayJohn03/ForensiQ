05 · blame ranking

The slow leak — statistical drift

χ² p = 0.003 < α = 0.01 → ALARM “has the failure mix shifted more than luck allows?” — a calibrated p-value, not a guessed threshold.
2-of-3 trailing windows must agree — one noisy window can neither fire the alarm nor reset it.
CUSUM watches each failure class for slow ramps the window is too blunt to feel.
Honest caveat: drift measures change, not badness — a deliberate change fires too, for a human to bless.
statistical drift A gradual shift in the failure mix — not a crash, a leak.
chi-square test Asks whether counts differ from baseline more than sampling noise allows.
p-value The odds of seeing this shift if nothing had changed. Small = probably real.
alpha (α) The chosen false-alarm tolerance — here 1%. Alerts fire only below it.
CUSUM A running total of small deviations — built to notice slow ramps early.

Counterfactual replay stays honest: “would top_k = 10 fix it?” — no. Marginal hits dilute the mean score, and an empty index stays empty. It’s an estimate, and it says so.
planted ground truth Failures inserted on purpose, pre-labelled — so detectors are scored against the key.
precision Of everything flagged, how much was real. High precision = no crying wolf.
recall Of everything real, how much was caught. High recall = nothing slipped past.
counterfactual replay Re-run the failure with one knob changed — and report the predicted effect honestly.
